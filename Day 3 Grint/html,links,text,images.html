Joker

The Joker is the character in DC universe written by 'Bill Finger'with artist 'Bob Kane'.
It Was designed in 1940. The Character Was Designed By 'Jerry Robinson'.
The exact credit is disputed, but DC officially acknowledges all three as co-creators of the iconic villain.Learn more

Key Facts About Joker’s about Creation

  • First Appearance: Batman #1 (1940)
  • Created By:
    1. Bill Finger
    2. Bob Kane
    3. Jerry Robinson
  • Dispute:
    1. Robinson claimed he designed the Joker’s look.
    2. Kane argued he based the character on Conrad Veidt’s role in The Man Who Laughs (1928).
    3. Finger’s role was primarily in shaping the narrative.
  • DC’s Official Position: All three are credited together in modern publications.Learn more

Joker’s Evolution

  • 1940's: Introduced as a homicidal psychopath with a sadistic sense of humor.
  • 1950's–60's: Shifted into a campy prankster due to Comics Code Authority restrictions.
  • 1970's: Revitalized by writer Denny O’Neil and artist Neal Adams as a darker, menacing killer.
  • 1980's: Alan Moore’s The Killing Joke (1988) gave him a complex, ambiguous origin.Learn more

Why The Dispute Matters

The Joker’s creation is one of the longest-running debates in comic history. It highlights:

  • Collaborative nature of comics – writers, artists, and designers all shape characters.
  • Legacy disputes – Kane often took credit for Batman-related creations, while Finger and Robinson’s contributions were under-recognized until later decades.

In short: The Joker was co-created by Bill Finger, Bob Kane, and Jerry Robinson in Batman #1 (1940). While the exact origin of his design is contested, DC today credits all three equally.
